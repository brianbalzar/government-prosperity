READING THE EVIDENCE

Sources & method

What spending means

Total government expenditure as a percentage of GDP from the IMF’s Public Finances in Modern History, via Our World in Data (IMF 2025 edition; OWID updated June 12, 2026). This broad fiscal measure includes transfers and interest where represented by the source, rather than only final consumption purchases. It is not a measure of taxes, deficits, debt, regulation, or government effectiveness.

What prosperity means here

GDP per capita, PPP, constant 2021 international dollars: World Bank WDI indicator NY.GDP.PCAP.PP.KD, via OWID (World Bank, Eurostat, OECD and IMF; OWID updated July 27, 2026). PPP adjusts for price differences between countries; constant prices adjust for inflation. This is average output per person, not total GDP, household income, inequality, or quality of life.

Matching & statistical comparison

OLS fits spending share = a + b × ln(GDP per capita), giving each country equal weight. R² describes the variation in spending shares associated with this fit. Group filters refit the line to included countries; chart scales remain fixed for comparison. Country search pins an exact matching country and enables its group if necessary. The heatmap and line facets share the same filters. No causal effect can be inferred: institutions, demographics, resource revenues and other factors may influence both measures.

Coverage & historical caution

Fiscal coverage varies by country and year. The IMF series uses general government where available—including central, state and local governments and social-security funds—and central government otherwise. Country-year coverage flags are not included in this OWID chart export, so individual dots are not claimed to have identical coverage. Definition changes may affect trends. The 2020 movement is retained without smoothing; both expenditure and changes in the GDP denominator can move the share. Shares can exceed 100% when expenditure exceeds annual GDP.

Annual-change heatmap method

Annual change equals spending share in year t minus spending share in year t−1, measured in percentage points, rather than percent growth. It requires observations in both consecutive years; the retained series starts in 2000, so that year has no annual-change value. Differences are rounded to six decimal places to avoid floating-point artifacts. The diverging color scale is fixed at −10 to +10 pp, with zero at its neutral midpoint; values beyond those limits share the endpoint colors and retain their exact values in tooltips. Blue indicates decreases and orange increases, without a good/bad judgment. The right-hand column and Change sorting always use total 2000–2024 change. CSV downloads contain the original spending levels used to calculate annual changes.
